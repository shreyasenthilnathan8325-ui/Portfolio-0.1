Story

How I got here

Background

I grew up in Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu, where I completed my schooling at PSBB Millennium School before pursuing a Bachelor of Commerce at Kumaraguru College of Liberal Arts and Science. My interest in commerce gradually developed into a curiosity about why people make the decisions they do, particularly when choosing products, responding to advertisements, or developing preferences for certain brands. During my undergraduate studies, I began to see business as more than numbers, transactions, and strategies. I became increasingly interested in the human behaviour behind them.

My interest in research grew as I explored topics connecting marketing, psychology, and technology. Working on research related to neuromarketing, emotional responses, and intelligent purchase decision-making in the fast-moving consumer goods sector encouraged me to look beyond conventional explanations of consumer behaviour. I wanted to understand not only what influences a purchase, but also how emotions, cognitive processes, and subconscious responses shape decisions. This curiosity has gradually shaped my academic direction and encouraged me to pursue a deeper understanding of consumer psychology through a business and marketing perspective.

The decision to go abroad

I want to study abroad because I am looking for an academic environment where research is an integral part of learning, rather than something pursued only after completing theoretical coursework. My goal is to develop a strong foundation in consumer behaviour, behavioural marketing, marketing research, and experimental methods, with opportunities to work with empirical data and learn how consumer decisions can be investigated systematically. I am particularly interested in research that connects marketing with behavioural science and consumer neuroscience while remaining grounded in business applications.

I also see postgraduate study as an important step towards my long-term ambition of pursuing a PhD. Studying in an international research environment would allow me to learn from different academic perspectives, strengthen my quantitative and analytical skills, and understand how research findings can be applied to real-world marketing challenges. I am therefore looking for a programme that offers meaningful research training, academic mentorship, and the opportunity to develop a focused research interest, rather than choosing a university solely for its reputation or location.

What I value

I value curiosity, consistency, intellectual honesty, and the willingness to learn from mistakes. I prefer understanding a concept thoroughly over simply memorising it, and I believe good research requires patience, evidence, and a willingness to question one's own assumptions. I am open to constructive criticism and recognise that developing as a researcher means accepting uncertainty and improving through feedback. Above all, I want to build a career in which my work contributes to a better understanding of people, their decisions, and the factors that shape their behaviour.
